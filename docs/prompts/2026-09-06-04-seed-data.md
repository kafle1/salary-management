# 2026-09-06 — the seed

## Prompt

> Write a deterministic seed producing 10,000 employees across 8 countries, 8 departments and 10
> roles.
>
> - One `random.Random(seed)` instance, no module level `random` calls, no `uuid4`, no
>   `datetime.now`. Two runs must produce identical rows.
> - Split it into a pure generator that yields dicts and a writer that inserts them, so
>   determinism can be tested without a database.
> - Pay is a role band in USD, multiplied by a country cost factor, jittered, then divided by the
>   exchange rate to get the local amount. Not a uniform random number.
> - Insert with `executemany` in batches, not 10,000 ORM objects.

## What I kept

The generator/writer split, which is the reason the determinism tests are unit tests and run in
milliseconds.

## What I rejected

- **Faker.** It was the first suggestion. It is a dependency, it is another seeding surface to
  pin, and I need about 95 names. A tuple of first names and a tuple of last names is the whole
  requirement.
- **Uniform salaries.** The first draft drew pay from `uniform(30000, 200000)`. That produces a
  dataset where the mean and the median are the same number and the dashboard has nothing to say.
  Replaced with a seniority pyramid, so the median lands roughly 25% below the mean, which is what
  real payroll looks like and what makes showing both worth doing.
- **`uuid4` for the email local part.** Non-deterministic, and it made every name in the UI
  unreadable. The row index guarantees uniqueness and costs nothing.
- **Deliberate outliers and pay-compression cases.** These were in my own first draft of the
  requirements, back when the product had an outlier report. The reviewer scoped that out and the
  data does not need to carry props for a feature that is not being built.

## One decision worth writing down

The seeded rates are all chosen so that a converted salary lands exactly on a cent. That is not
cosmetic: it means the sum of the rounded rows and the rounded sum of the rows are the same
number, so the KPI card, the country breakdown and a hand check all agree. A rate that does not
have that property is a rounding policy question and it is the sort of thing that turns into a
finance ticket six months later. There is a comment on the table saying so.
